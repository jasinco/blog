# Term
Predicates: a proposition with parameter $x>0$
Propositional function: $P(x)$
Compound Expression: propositional logic with predicates $P(3) and P(-1)$

# Quantifier
## Domain
$U$
## Universal
$$
P(x): x>0 \
forall x(P(x))
$$
for all x, P(x)

## Existential 
or there exists
$$
exists x(P(x))
$$
for some x, P(x)

## Uniqueness (Optional)
$$
exists x (P(x)) = exists x (P(x) and forall y (P(y) → y=x ))
$$
there is a unique x, one and only one

## Examples
U = all people
S(x) = student
H(x) = study hard
G(x) = High score
### No one studies hard
$forall x (not H(x))$

### All students study hard
$forall x (S(x) → H(x))$

### Some students get high score
$exists x(S(x) and G(x))$

### If any student studies hard then he will get a high score
$forall x(S(x) and H(x) → G(x))$

### No student gets a high score
$not exists x((G(x) and S(x)))$

## Examples 2
* All lions are fierce  
* Some lions do not drink coffee
* Some fierce creatures do not drink coffee

logics:  
U=creature  
$L(x)$ is lion  
$F(x)$ is fierce  
$C(x)$ drink coffee  

$forall x(L(x) → F(x))$
$exists x(L(x) and not C(x))$
$exists x(F(x) and not C(x))$

# Equiv in Predicate logic
1. same truth value
$forall x (P(x) and Q(x)) equiv forall x P(x) and forall x Q(x)$

Statements involving predicates and quantifeirs are logically equivalent if and only if they have the same truth value no matter which predicates are substituted into these statements and which domain of discourse is used for the variables in these propositional functions. We use the notation S ≡ T to indicate that two statements S and T involving predicates and quantifeirs are logically equivalent.
